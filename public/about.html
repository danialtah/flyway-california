    About FlyWay California

    FlyWay California helps you find rare and migratory birds in California. It shows this week's notable sightings on a list and a map, lets you look near you, has a page for every county, and has a page for every bird on the state list. It's a free, non-commercial project.

    What you'll find

    • Notable sightings: birds that eBird flags as unusual for the place or the time of year, reported in the last 7 days. Only confirmed reports are shown. The list updates every 15 minutes.
    • Rarest birds: this week's notable birds ranked from rarest to least rare, for all of California, one county, or near you.
    • Rare bird hotspots: the eBird hotspots with the most rare birds reported recently.
    • Notable sightings by rarity: every notable bird, grouped as Rare (no seasonal data), Rare, Uncommon, Common, and Escapee or non-native. Hybrids are grouped with Common. Escapees and non-native birds are birds that aren't on the official California list, like escaped pets.
    • County pages: one county's notable sightings from the last 7 or 30 days, with its rarest birds and hotspots.
    • California Bird Species: every bird recorded in California, A to Z, with filters for rarity, season, and CBRC review species.
    • Species pages: each bird's status, when it's in California, and its recent sightings on a map.
    • Search: find a bird, county, or place from the top of any page.

    What the labels mean

    Rarity

    Rarity comes from eBird Status and Trends: how likely birders are to find the species in its best month, averaged across California.

    • Common: found on 5% or more of checklists.
    • Uncommon: found on 0.1% to 5% of checklists.
    • Rare: found on fewer than 0.1% of checklists. A few species were adjusted to match eBird's own labels.
    • Rare (no seasonal data): Status and Trends has no California data for the species, usually because it's a vagrant that rarely reaches the state. These are often the rarest birds of all.

    Migration

    • Year-round: in California in every season.
    • Winter visitor: mostly here in the colder months.
    • Summer visitor: mostly here in the warmer months, often to breed.
    • Migrant: passes through in spring or fall.
    • Rare visitor / vagrant: a wild bird that rarely reaches California, too rare for eBird Status and Trends to show a season.

    CBRC review species

    The California Bird Records Committee reviews sightings of these species. If you see one, submit photos or notes.

    Privacy

    • Sightings at private locations, like someone's home, show only the county. They never get a place name or a map pin.
    • "Use my location" stays in your browser for the map and lists. When you search for a bird near you, your location, rounded to about 1 km, is sent to eBird to find nearby sightings.
    • What you type in the search at the top of the page is sent to FlyWay California's server to suggest matching eBird hotspots.
    • A place you type is sent to OpenStreetMap's place search to find it on the map.
    • The site has no accounts, ads, or tracking.

    Photos

    FlyWay California doesn't host bird photos. Each species page links to eBird, where you can see licensed photos from the Macaulay Library.
